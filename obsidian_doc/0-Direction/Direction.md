## Mission

Make coaching effortless to run and effortless to find. Coaches focus on coaching, not admin. Athletes find the right coach for their needs and book in seconds.

## Vision

Become the default way people find, book, and manage sports coaching in their city.

---

## The Problem

Three personas, three broken workflows, all connected.

### Coach

The coach's day is spent more on coordination than coaching.

- **Manual confirmation loop.** Groups: the coach messages each WhatsApp group "who's coming tomorrow?" and waits. Individual: SMS each client the day before. Someone cancels at 9pm — coach scrambles to find a replacement manually.
- **Gaps in the schedule bleed money.** A client cancels an individual lesson — the coach sits idle for 45 minutes (at the pool, on the court) because there's no way to automatically offer that slot to someone else. No one to message, no system to shift the next client earlier. "Skaranie boskie."
- **No centralized view.** Cancellations, postponements, confirmations — scattered across WhatsApp, SMS, and memory. The coach mentally tracks who's in, who's out, who's flaky.
- **Clients disappear silently.** Athletes stop showing up without saying why. The coach doesn't track who dropped off, doesn't follow up, and loses revenue without realizing it. No CRM, no nudge, no re-engagement.
- **Finding new athletes costs money or luck.** Coaches pay for Instagram/Facebook geo-targeted ads or rely on referrals. No structured way to be discovered organically. Some coaches spend on portals (ActiveNow ~100 PLN/month) that underdeliver.
- **Existing tools are bad.** Coaches have tried scheduling apps (ActiveNow) — they pay ~100 PLN/month but deal with missing notifications, no auto-reschedule, poor UX. Many are actively looking for alternatives.

### School owner

The school sees its coaches through a fog.

- **Coaches' availability is never up to date.** Coaches message the school when something changes — but often they don't. The school owner doesn't know which slots are booked, which hours are free, which coaches have capacity.
- **No shared calendar.** Each coach keeps their own schedule (phone, head, maybe an app). The school has no cross-coach view. Conflicts, double-bookings, and wasted venue time happen regularly.
- **Communication overhead.** The school owner spends time chasing coaches for schedule updates instead of running the business.

### Athlete

Finding and booking a coach is stuck in the phone-a-friend era.

- **No way to discover coaches.** No platform to search "swimming coach in my area" with ratings, reviews, and availability. Athletes ask friends, google randomly, or pick whoever answers the phone.
- **No quality signal.** No rankings, no reviews, no way to compare. You pick a coach and hope they're good.
- **Booking is back-and-forth messaging.** "Do you have availability Thursday?" → wait → "No, how about Friday?" → wait → "What time?" Three days to book one training.
- **No visibility into availability.** Athletes can't see open slots. If they're nearby and free, they can't just browse and book — they have to message and wait.
- **Group coordination falls on the athlete too.** Coach asks "who's coming?" in WhatsApp — athletes have to remember to respond. Makeup sessions (training with a different group) require manual coordination.

### How they connect

These aren't three separate problems — they're the same broken system.

```
Coach can't fill hours ← Athletes can't find coaches
School can't see availability ← Coach has no shared calendar
Athletes coordinate in WhatsApp ← Coach manages via WhatsApp
Everyone loses time ← No single system connects them
```

Sessio sits in the middle. One tool that gives the coach a calendar others can see, the school a dashboard, and the athlete a way to find, book, and confirm.

## Why Now — and Why Doesn't This Exist Yet?

The pain is obvious. Every coach feels it. So why is there no Doctolib for sports coaching — and what changed?

### Why it didn't exist

- **Market looks niche from the outside.** VCs see "tennis coaching app" and pass. But it's every sport, every city, every coach running groups or individual lessons. Massive market, just fragmented. The smart money went to fitness apps and wellness marketplaces instead.
- **Cold-start problem killed every attempt.** HOKALI, CoachUp — all tried to be marketplaces from day one. Recruit coaches AND athletes simultaneously. No density, no value, dead. No one thought to start as a tool the coach brings to existing clients.
- **Booking tools solve the wrong problem.** Calendly, Booksy, SimplyBook — built for 1-on-1 appointments. Sports coaching has group rosters, backfill logic, waitlists, recurring schedules, and auto-reschedule needs. None of the general tools handle this.
- **WhatsApp is "good enough" — until it isn't.** A coach with 2 groups manages fine. At 10+ groups and 40+ athletes, it's hell. But by then they've built WhatsApp habits for years. No trigger moment — they just suffer more each month.
- **Existing sports tools are bad.** ActiveNow (Poland, swimming) charges ~100 PLN/month but has no cancel notifications, no auto-reschedule, poor UX. Coaches pay and are unhappy. The bar is low.

### Why now

1. **The market already pays.** ActiveNow proves coaches spend ~100 PLN/month on scheduling tools. Demand exists — it's just underserved.
2. **Incumbents are bad and complacent.** Missing notifications, no auto-reschedule, poor UX. Coaches are unhappy but stuck. Low bar to beat.
3. **Booking behavior is trained.** Doctolib trained France, Booksy trained Poland. People expect to book everything online. Sports coaching is one of the last holdouts.

**Competition is weak.** Marketplaces (CoachUp, HOKALI) cold-started and died. Tools exist (ActiveNow, CoachNow) but have no discovery layer and bad UX. Court platforms (Playtomic) serve venues, not coaches. The closest competitor (CoachIQ) is US-only and tiny. ## Our Approach

**Start as a tool, become a marketplace.**

1. **Tool first.** Replace the coach's WhatsApp coordination with one app. Auto-confirm, auto-backfill, shared calendar. The coach brings their own clients — no cold-start problem.
2. **School support from day one.** Schools are our first users (direct access). Give the school owner visibility into all their coaches. This alone is valuable.
3. **Discovery built in, not bolted on.** Coach profiles, ratings, and reviews are part of the product from the start. As coaches onboard and athletes rate them, the marketplace builds itself organically.
4. **Hyper-local density.** Start with coaches in Warsaw. One city. Expand only after proving the tool works and coaches adopt it for daily use.

**Unfair advantages:**

1. **The tool solves cold start.** Every coaching marketplace died trying to recruit both sides at once. Sessio doesn't need a marketplace to be useful on day one — the scheduling tool has standalone value. Coach brings existing clients. Clients are now on the platform. Marketplace builds itself from real usage, not from scratch.

2. **Every coach is a distribution channel.** One coach onboards 20-40 athletes. Those athletes see other coaches on the platform. Each coach multiplies the demand side without Sessio spending on acquisition. The more coaches join, the more athletes arrive for free.

3. **Switching cost compounds over time.** Schedule history, client relationships, reviews, ratings — all live in Sessio. Going back to WhatsApp means losing everything. ActiveNow doesn't have this problem (for them) because they have no marketplace layer. With Sessio, the more you use it, the harder it is to leave.

4. **Network effects at city level.** Once there are 20+ coaches in one city, athletes start finding coaches through the platform instead of paid Instagram ads. Coaches stop paying for ads because Sessio is their lead source. At that point, leaving means losing new clients — not just a tool.

5. **Europe is empty.** CoachIQ just raised $1.3M for the similiar positioning — US only. No one is building this in Europe, where booking behavior is already trained (Booksy in Poland, Doctolib in France). First mover advantage is real when the market is ready but unoccupied.

## Key Bets

Things we believe but haven't fully proven yet:

1. **Coach-first is the way in.** Every previous attempt (CoachUp, HOKALI) tried to build a consumer marketplace first — recruit athletes, then hope coaches follow. All failed. Our bet: start with coaches. Give them a dead-simple scheduling tool (confirm, cancel, reschedule, client communication). They bring their own clients. Clients arrive on the platform organically. The marketplace grows from the supply side, not the demand side. — *This is the central bet. If this sequencing is wrong, nothing else matters.*

2. **Coaches are underserved with basic tooling.** They don't need a fancy platform — just reliable scheduling, automated confirmations, cancel/reschedule flows, and client communication without WhatsApp chaos. The bar is low. — *Signal: Czarek paid ~100 PLN/month for ActiveNow and it didn't even send cancel notifications. His colleague says many coaches would switch. The tool doesn't need to be revolutionary — just good.*

3. **Automatic slot-filling is the feature coaches can't build themselves.** When someone cancels, the system proposes the empty spot to other people — no coach intervention. For group coaches (Kajtek/tennis): spot opens → waitlist or people from other groups get notified → they actually take it. For individual coaches (Czarek/swimming): freed slot gets offered to other clients, or next client gets asked to come earlier. Same system, two use cases. — *Signal: Kajtek says people regularly switch groups when offered. Czarek called gap-filling "skaranie boskie" and repeated it as his #1 ask.*

4. **Schools will pay for a single source of truth.** Multi-coach schools have no shared calendar, no cross-coach visibility. They chase coaches for schedule updates. A unified dashboard with real-time availability is worth paying for. ~50 EUR/month for a small school, scaling up with number of coaches/clients. — *Signal: ActiveNow charges 60-1500 PLN/month and schools pay it. The willingness is there — the product just needs to be better.*

5. **Athletes have nowhere to find and compare coaches.** No reviews, no ratings, no way to search "swimming coach in my area" and make an informed choice. It's word of mouth or random Google results. — *Signal: coaches pay for Instagram/Facebook ads because organic discovery doesn't exist. The demand is there — there's just no platform to serve it.*
