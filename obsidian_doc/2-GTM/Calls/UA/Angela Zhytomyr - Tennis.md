## How she coordinates

- She color-codes her sessions in EasyWeek to distinguish from regular court rentals

## Cancellation policy

- **24-hour free cancellation** by default
- Flexible for genuine reasons (illness, air raids) — they accommodate
- In practice, clients don't abuse the policy
- Clients prefer to **reschedule rather than cancel** when possible
- Late cancellations: client still pays for the court + training

## Client acquisition

- Club runs **Instagram** — posts, videos, tournaments, activity content
- **Telegram channel** — news, rate changes, outdoor court openings, tournament announcements
- People come through Instagram, then **word of mouth / recommendations**

## Reaction to backfill/happy hour concept

| Topic | Reaction |
|-------|----------|
| Discount to fill cancelled slot | **Not interested** — "I'd rather rest that hour" |
| Last-minute fill problem | Not a real pain — late cancellers still pay |
| App for scheduling | Already uses EasyWeek for courts, no expressed need for more |

## Surprising insights

1. **No backfill pain.** Because late cancellers still pay (court + training), she doesn't lose money on no-shows. The financial incentive to fill empty slots doesn't exist for her.
2. **Part-time = different priorities.** At 10-15 hours/week, she's not trying to maximize every hour. A gap is a rest, not lost income.
3. **EasyWeek solves the court problem, not the coaching problem.** The app is purely for venue booking visibility. Coaching coordination is still manual chat.
4. **Club context matters.** She's not an independent coach hustling for clients — she's embedded in a club with its own marketing (Instagram, Telegram). Lead gen is a club function, not an individual one.

## Next step

- Useful as a data point: part-time club coaches may not be the right persona
