## TL;DR

Three concrete objections, ranked by Maxime's emphasis:

1. **"Why now" is not convincing.** No clear 2025 catalyst (law change, AI, consumer behavior shift). Not a disqualifier on its own, but a red flag — absence of a why-now often means the market already has good-enough solutions or there's a structural reason nobody has won.
2. **Consumer GTM is not covered.** Consumer (athlete) side is the main value driver — that's where the millions of users and the commission revenue come from. Relying on coaches to bring their community is not a consumer acquisition strategy. Needs direct consumer channels: content, campaigns, brand building.
3. **Pivot the pitch away from "solve coach admin" toward "fill empty spots."** Admin is already solved by dozens of tools. The differentiated unlock is connecting booking to a marketplace that fills underutilized inventory.

Secondary: founder-idea fit was "okay, not the strongest" — but not Maxime's biggest worry. Family situation is not a concern if execution is there.

## The "why now" problem — what Maxime is actually asking

He gave the Booksy / beauty-salon booking example unprompted: *"These platforms existed 10-15 years ago but only became big 2-3-4 years ago. The players winning now have been in the space for 10-15 years."*

His framing for what counts as a why now:
- Law change
- AI
- Consumer behavior shift (*"people are booking more now than before for some reason"*)
- Demographic shift (*"certain population are more digital now"*)

**Implication:** Maxime is not asking for hype — he's asking for a structural story that explains why a solution that could have existed forever will actually get adopted NOW. Without one, he worries the market is already served well enough.

---

## Action items

- [ ] Develop a genuine why-now narrative — honest, not retrofit AI-slop. Leading candidates: (a) AI as assistant changes coach adoption economics, (b) WhatsApp chaos ripened into an acute pain, (c) post-COVID shift to fluid group/happy-hour lesson structures that didn't exist in 2013.
- [ ] Reframe pitch: lead with **"we fill underutilized inventory"** (marketplace / happy-hour / backfill), not **"we clean up coach admin."** Admin is already solved and is not a wedge.
- [ ] Build a direct consumer GTM plan: content, campaigns, community. Cannot rely solely on coaches bringing their athletes. Show investors the beginning of a consumer acquisition curve.
- [ ] Validate the thesis in 1–2 months with Warsaw MVP. Count how many hours per week each coach could fill but currently can't. If 3–5 hours/coach/week → thesis holds. If closer to zero → wrong wedge.
- [ ] Read Andrew Chen, *The Cold Start Problem* — atomic-network approach to marketplace bootstrapping. Maxime recommended it directly.
- [ ] Concentrate density. Warsaw only. Possibly one district only. Don't spread across cities.
- [ ] If traction comes through: re-contact Maxime for intros. Door explicitly left open.

## Maxime's validation path (direct advice)

> *"Your thing is easy to validate. Spend a month, two months, talk to coaches, bring them on an MVP, see if you get first traction. Put timelines to confirm stuff and validate quickly. Talk to users — is there willingness to pay? Is there value behind it or is it just nice to have?"*

## Open question this raised for me

Maxime split coaches (prosumers) from athletes (consumers) and said the consumers are where the real growth has to come from. This clashes with our current positioning (tool-first for coaches, marketplace grows from there). Either:

1. He's right and the positioning needs to flip → consumer brand first, coaches as supply.
2. He's misreading the model — the tool-first approach is specifically designed to avoid the disintermediation problem (coaches bring existing relationships; we monetize only new leads). In that case the tool-first pitch needs to be communicated much more clearly because the model only works if Sessio is the operating system, not the middleman.

Worth deciding which it is before the next investor conversation.
